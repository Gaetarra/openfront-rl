### Why 1v1 collapsed: the modes' training signals were coupled

This is the user's reading, and the evidence agrees. In PPO the three modes' rewards reached the shared network
together in three ways.
- **The value baseline.** One value head V(s) was fit to every mode's returns, so each mode's GAE advantages rest on a
  baseline shaped by the others. Over all modes' games, the value head's mid-game outcome correlation fell from 0.72
  in 4N's first quarter to 0.49–0.55 after. League v2, on 1v1 only, held 0.76–0.82.
- **Advantage normalisation.** Each minibatch normalises Â = (A − μ)/σ over all its rows, and ~75% of them came from
  FFA (mean reward flat at −0.86) or Humans vs Nations. Those rows set μ and σ, so the other modes' rewards shift and
  rescale every 1v1 advantage.
- **One summed gradient.** Every row's gradient adds into one policy update, and the network did not keep the modes
  apart.
  - The MoE router used the same 3 of its 8 experts (0, 1, 5) in every mode, and the other 5 got 1–6% each. This is
    from a gate probe of 6 games × 500 decisions with the start policy acting.
  - From the gates alone the mode is guessed 51% of the time at the start and 59% after 4N, against 33% for chance.
  - 4N moved those experts the same way in every mode (expert 0 down, expert 5 up).
  - The 1v1 attack share drifted from 12.1% to 10.0%, toward FFA's. League v2 held 12–14%.

**Measured cost:** with 25% of the rows from 1v1, on their own ±1 rewards, the averages ended ~300 Elo below the start.
The KL to the 3B clone stayed ~0.037, so the leash did not protect 1v1.

**If modes share a PPO run again,** the fixes follow from the three couplings: per-mode advantage normalisation,
per-mode value targets (PopArt, or one value head per mode), and a check of each mode's share of the gradient.

**No indexing bug was found.** `trainer/ppo.py` (`seat_rows`, rollout), `trainer/env.py`, `bridge/worker.ts`,
`bridge/game.ts` and `bridge/ranking.ts` pair each 1v1 learner row with its own seat's observation, action and reward.
A control run checks the mixed code path before the next mixed run (deferred, below).

### Other findings of this session

All read-only.
- **Mechanics checked (v0.34.24).**
  - **Troops:** one pool defends all of a player's land.
    - An enemy's time per tile scales with home troops ÷ attack troops, clamped to 0.82–7.5.
    - Its loss per tile scales with the same ratio, clamped to 0.6–2, times a base plus the defender's troops per tile
      (`Config.attackLogic`).
    - Sending an attack removes its troops at once (`AttackExecution.ts:117-123`).
    - Growth peaks at ~42% of the cap.
  - **Economy:** land makes no gold (a flat 1,000/s); trade and trains are the economy.
  - **Kills:** the attack that pushes a player under 100 tiles takes the kill, the gold (all of a nation's, half of a
    human's) and the remaining land, so kills can be stolen. A nuke kill credits nobody.
  - **Nukes:** losing 10% of the land to a nuke costs ~41% of the troops.
  - **Warships:** they capture trade ships (paid when the ship reaches the captor's port, at most ~125k a ship) and
    sink a boat with one shell within 130 tiles.
- **v0.35.2 scan.**
  - **The engine moved.** `src/core` became `packages/{engine,engine-lib,engine-api,shared}`; 20 bridge and script
    files import it.
  - **Simulation changes:**
    - trade ship and train spawn rates;
    - water-path tie-breaks;
    - a train fix;
    - nukes annex cut-off land again;
    - a MIRV fix;
    - a large nation AI rewrite for Medium and harder, which also covers the tribes' boat routes.
  - **Unchanged:** attack formulas, growth, gold, costs, warships, SAMs and intents.
  - **New:** Ranked FFA, Ranked Clan Wars, game snapshots, lobbies up to ~210 players, and no mutable statics left.
  - **Ranked FFA:** 4–10 players, nations off, 400 tribes, 20 minutes, the last human wins.
    - Its maps are Europe, Asia, Africa, North and South America, and Australia.
    - Only Asia and Australia fit the 192 × 256 grid.
  - **Clan Wars:** 2–5 a side, up to 4 clans, donations on.
